Executive Summary

Funding Tier is a front-end enrollment company in consumer debt relief. It qualifies callers, matches each one to the best-fit program, enrolls them, and is paid by the servicing partner as the client pays. This proposal asks for $90,000 for a 30% membership interest, on terms designed so the investor is repaid first and the company stays simple to run.

What The Company Does

Every call is qualified for ability to pay, soft-credit-pulled and routed to one of three debt programs — settlement, validation, resolution — with tax resolution available on the same call. Funding Tier never holds client funds; servicing partners run the programs and remit a contractual share of each client payment, so revenue arrives behind enrollment and compounds as cohorts stack.

What Has Been Built

A deal router and state-aware scripts for every program; a recruiting-to-training pipeline with AI résumé screening and state hiring rules; a pay and reconciliation engine that matches every remittance to a deal and produces the monthly commission run the CPA posts from; and an operating model that simulates staffing, call sources, program mix, attrition and cash month by month. The investor receives live access to all of it.

Operating Environment

Debt relief marketing is regulated at federal and state level (telemarketing sales rules, state licensing and fee rules, prescreen credit rules for mail). Funding Tier's position is deliberately narrow: it is paid by partners, never by consumers; scripts carry the required disclosures; the router enforces state exclusions before a quote; prescreen mail runs through a lender-partner firm-offer structure; and attorney review precedes any new creative. Spreading enrollments across four programs and partners limits how much any single rule or partner change can affect revenue.

$167 = $251 − $80 maintenance − $4 processing. Or accelerated: 90% × 7 months, then 25% to month 24.
The survival weighting (safe case): 60% of enrollments clear their first payment; of those, 15% cancel each month. So the share of files still paying is 60% in month 1, 51% in month 2, 43% in month 3, 37% in month 4, 10% by month 12 and about 1% by month 24. Every scheduled payout is multiplied by that share, which is why a $4,256 Validation payout books as $739. Rep commission and bonuses apply only to deals closed by the owner-operator or a US W-2 closer and carry the same weighting; offshore closers are paid by the hour, so their deals keep the full expected payout. Blended at today's 33 / 40 / 27 mix, one $15,000 deal nets $524 when US-closed and $683 when offshore-closed. At 6% monthly cancellation: $892 and $1,100.

Reading The Numbers

Revenue arrives behind the deals — Debt Settlement pays at month 2, Validation and Resolution build month over month — so the first two months are negative in every configuration. That gap, $17,894 at its deepest for Option C, is what the capital protects; the bank never falls below $72,106.

Reserve target (six months of overhead plus chargeback buffer) is met in month 4; the first hire trigger fires in month 4. The month-24 bank figure is before the distribution waterfall is applied.

Three offshore closers (C) produce roughly twice the volume of one US closer (B), and their deals carry no commission or bonuses — only their hourly labor — which is why C's net is more than three times B's. An office (F) costs about $3,350 a month of profit for the same output. Direct mail (D) adds deals but needs the Tier 1 test to prove its response rate before it earns a standing budget.
