Executive Summary And Mission

Funding Tier is a front-end enrollment company for consumer debt relief. It finds people who need help, qualifies them for ability to pay, enrolls them in the right program, and is paid by the servicing partner as the client pays.

Mission — a win for everybody. The client gets a program they can afford and understand. The closer is paid fairly and on time. The servicing partner receives a qualified file that stays. The owners build a company that grows into a full front and back end with proper marketing and proper capital — on a system that already runs the business today.

  • Partner-paid; Funding Tier never holds client funds
  • Revenue arrives 1–2 months after enrollment and compounds as monthly cohorts stack
  • Fixed cost stays low: virtual, offshore closers, software already built

  • Owner + 3 Egypt closers at $12 / hour (CallBlade, referred by Shield Services)
  • Live transfers at $14 per call, 2-minute buffer, fill every seat
  • At least 85% Shield Services for the first 90 days, 80% of them as buyouts — cash from month 2, then the standing mix
  • Mail and social added after the reserve is met, capped at 50% of volume
What Decides The Outcome
  • Close rate (15% safe, 16% target)
  • First-payment rate (60% safe, 70% target)
  • Monthly cancellation on perpetual files (15% safe, 10% target)
  • Each is measured weekly and the model re-run with actuals

How The Election Works
  • The election is made per file. The buyout turns a 36-month stream into one payment and ends chargeback exposure on that file; the perpetual keeps the stream and the risk.
  • Section 11 shows how many months the perpetual needs to pass the buyout at each cancellation rate (10 months at 15% on $15k; never at 10%+ on $25k).
  • Remittance: partners remit in arrears by the 15th of the following month. The reconciliation engine matches every line to its deal, flags NSFs and cancellations, and sets each deal's expected pay date.

SWOT Analysis

An honest reading of where Funding Tier is strong, where it is thin, what the market offers and what could hurt. Each item is paired with what the plan does about it.

Strengths

  • System already built — router, scripts, QC, pay engine, model; no development spend in the plan
  • Partner-paid, four programs — no client funds, broad state coverage, a lead rarely wasted
  • Credit data — TransUnion gateway built; Array credit-parameter check available to cut backend pull costs
  • Low fixed cost — virtual, $12 / hour closers, E&O in place
  • Operator who closes — the founder sells, trains and builds

Weaknesses

  • Single operator at launch — capacity caps at 3 closers until a team lead is added (rule: 4th closer)
  • Revenue lag — month 1 is paid before any payout; reserve target takes ~3 months
  • Retention is assumed until measured — hence a deliberately safe case and weekly measurement
  • No mail history — the first drop is a test, sized to the reserve
  • Partner terms are contractual and can change at renewal

Opportunities

  • Record consumer debt and delinquency at a 10-year high
  • $25k+ households reachable through prescreen mail and social retargeting
  • Tax resolution attached to the same call — zero acquisition cost, modelled at zero
  • Own data campaigns (dialing, SMS, RVM) on coreg data through app.krestmarketing.com
  • Back end later — servicing in-house keeps the whole payout; treated as a separate financing

Threats

  • TCPA litigators — mitigated by pre-scrubbed lists, Blacklist Alliance and DNC in the system (§6)
  • State rule changes — router enforces exclusions; partners hold the licences
  • Transfer vendor quality and price — second vendor, weekly disputes, buffer mix by close rate
  • Larger competitors outbid on paid media — we buy transfers and mail $25k+ households instead
  • Economic shift that lowers first-pay rates — hold rule pauses hiring under 50%
  • Market saturation — people already enrolled elsewhere keep answering calls and ads looking for faster settlements or lower payments; a retention threat if they churn again, an opportunity when the router places them in a program that actually fits

Hiring US Closers — Rules, Costs And Where The Talent Is

What the law allows for commission pay, what a W-2 closer really costs on top of wages, and where experienced debt-relief closers already work.

Commission-Only And Draw Rules
  • Federal (FLSA): inside sales is non-exempt. A commission-only W-2 closer must still receive at least minimum wage for every hour in each workweek, plus overtime over 40 hours. The outside-sales exemption does not apply to phone sales; the retail "7(i)" overtime exemption does not fit a debt-relief front end.
  • Draw against commission: lawful everywhere as long as the draw itself meets minimum wage each pay period; recoverable draws (clawing back from future commission) are allowed under the FLSA but restricted in several states.
  • California: draw-only pay for inside sales is unlawful (Vaquero v. Stoneledge, 2017); rest breaks must be paid separately; daily overtime over 8 hours; the ABC test (AB 5) makes 1099 closers impractical.
  • Massachusetts: commission-only inside sales are owed separate overtime and Sunday premium pay (Sullivan v. Sleepy's).
  • New York: written commission agreement required (Labor Law §191).
  • 100% commission, no draw: only as an independent contractor (1099), only where the state uses a common-law or economic-realities test and the closer controls hours and tools. Safest: Texas, Florida, Arizona, Georgia, Tennessee, North Carolina, Utah, Nevada (modified ABC). Avoid: California, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Illinois, Connecticut, Vermont, Maine, New Mexico.

Marketing Plan

Transfers fill the seats; mail and social replace part of that volume with higher-intent, larger-debt files — a mail response is a household choosing a loan swap or debt relief, a social call is someone who went looking — so close rates and debt loads should run higher and lean toward settlement (marketing mix 50 / 30 / 20). Goal: 25% of volume from mail and social once the reserve is met; hard cap 50%. Every channel is tested at Tier 1 and scaled only on measured cost per enrolled file.

Retention Through Service

Retention and learning are the two levers that compound. Retention is the biggest lever in the plan — the difference between the safe and target cases is worth $35,849 a month on the same team. It rises with customer service, trust, a program the client understands, and partners who do their job.

Also illustrated inPlaybook §4 and §7

Qualify for ability to pay

  • Income verification on every file
  • Payment date set against the client's pay cycle
  • Disqualify fast and kindly — a file that will NSF costs more than no file

Explain the program in depth

  • Scripts walk the client through what happens in months 1, 2, 3 and at settlement
  • Required disclosures read, not rushed; QC scores comprehension
  • Welcome email and AI Genie quality call after enrollment on validation

Service and trust after the sale

  • Closer stays the client's contact for the first 60 days
  • Reminder before each draft; proactive call on any NSF
  • Complaints routed to the partner and tracked to close within 5 days

Hold the partners to it

  • Weekly reconciliation of first-drafts and cancellations by partner
  • A partner running 5 points worse than the others is reviewed
  • Changing a partner is a both-member action, backed by data

FAQ
  • Is the $90,000 spent on software? No — it funds closers and calls; the system is built.
  • When does Paul get paid? Once the reserve is met and the Manager's draw is paid, 100% of member distributions go to Paul until $90,000 is back — about month 7 on the safe case — then 30% of profit.
  • What if the Egypt closers fail the trial? Plan B (another BPO) or Plan C (a US closer on a draw); the system and the numbers are the same.
  • What if cancellations are worse than 15%? The buyout election caps the loss on validation files; the hold rule pauses hiring; the model is re-run with real rates.
  • Can Paul work in the business? Yes, in any role both members agree to in writing.
  • Who holds the clients' money? The servicing partners, never Funding Tier.
