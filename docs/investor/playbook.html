How We Run Funding Tier

One document for both members: who does what, the rhythm we keep, the rules money follows, how the sales floor stays clean, how marketing is tested before it is scaled, and the exact numbers that tell us when to hire and when to invest. Every rule here is measurable in app.krestmarketing.com or the pay engine.

Roles

Nathan — Manager

  • Sales floor, program partners, product and software, compliance, rep hiring, marketing within budget
  • SPIFFs and bonus amounts within budget; vendor selection and disputes
  • Runs the monthly close: reconciliation → commission run → hands the CPA the posting file

Paul — Member

  • Full information rights: live dashboard, bank view, P&L by the 15th
  • Co-signer on every account; second signature on the dual-signature list
  • Brings the CPA and HR provider; sponsor for the expansion-event decisions

CPA (Paul's)

  • Posts from the monthly commission run and the reconciled ledger — does not re-determine eligibility
  • Partnership return (1065 / K-1s), payroll tax compliance, quarterly tax-distribution estimate
  • View-only bank and dashboard access, under NDA

HR / payroll (Paul's)

  • W-2 onboarding and payroll for US closers (California daily overtime rules); contractor agreements for offshore
  • Never puts 1099 commission-only reps on a timeclock; follows the state hiring map
  • Runs only member-approved payroll batches; no standing debit authority
Operating Rhythm

Daily

  • Occupancy and transfers by vendor
  • Deals, first-pay dates set, QC flags
  • Unanswered inbound = zero

Weekly

  • Pipeline and rep scorecards
  • Vendor close rate and disputes
  • Recruiting stages moving

Monthly (by the 15th)

  • Reconciliation → commission run → CPA
  • P&L, bank vs reserve, triggers reviewed
  • Hire / invest / hold decision minuted

Quarterly

  • Tax distributions; founder bonus if due
  • Re-fit the model to measured first-pay and cancel rates
  • Reserve buffer and insurance review

Launch Checklist — The First Ninety Days

In order. Nothing on the sales side starts until the money side is locked, and nothing on the marketing side starts until the sales side has a measured baseline.

Weeks 1–2 · Paper and money
  • Mutual NDA signed; CPA, HR, attorney and any data vendor sign before access
  • Attorney engaged for Funding Tier LLC: MIPA, A&R operating agreement, exhibits
  • CPA confirms partnership classification, EIN and state filing; tax-distribution rate set
  • Operating account opened: both signers, dual control above $10,000, cards to members only
  • Tranche 1 ($30,000) received; 10% issued; Accounts Schedule filled in
  • Platform and hosting licence signed so the company holds durable rights to app.krestmarketing.com, hosting and code
Weeks 2–4 · Floor ready
  • Pick the launch roster: owner + 1 US closer, or owner + 3 offshore closers (the model shows both)
  • Offers out through the recruiting pipeline (application → AI screen → interview → NDA → training)
  • Dialer, DIDs, scripts, enrollment SOPs and the call-QC transcription live for every seat
  • Transfer vendors confirmed with buffer mix and dispute terms; credit-pull accounts active per program
  • Pay rules page versioned; first commission run scheduled; expected-pay-date logic on every deal
Weeks 4–8 · Baseline
  • Fill closers with transfers only; target occupancy ≥ 90% by week 6
  • Measure by vendor: close rate, cost per deal, first-pay clear, average debt
  • Program routing checked on every file: state eligibility, debt floor, ability to pay, buyout vs perpetual
  • First month-end close delivered to the CPA by the 15th; both members review bank vs reserve
Weeks 8–13 · Decide
  • Re-fit the model to the measured first-pay and cancellation rates — this is the first real forecast
  • If occupancy ≥ 90% two months running and bank ≥ reserve + 3 × seat cost: hire the next closer
  • If reserve is met: schedule the Tier 1 direct-mail test (page 4) against the kill criteria
  • Tranche 3 received; CPA and HR fully onboarded; first quarterly review booked
Rule of the first quarter: every dollar of the $90,000 that leaves the bank buys either a measured seat or a measured test. Nothing is spent on an assumption that has not been checked against the CRM.

Sales Floor And File Quality

Retention is worth more than any marketing lever: the same deal is worth two to three times as much when the client stays. These are the rules that protect it.

Who We Enroll
  • Stated employment, income and a monthly payment the client can show they can make — no file without an ability-to-pay check
  • Unsecured debt above the program floor; state eligible for the program quoted (state map in the toolkit)
  • No active bankruptcy; no recent NSF pattern the client cannot explain
  • Every enrollment call transcribed and scored by the AI call-QC; risk flags reviewed before the first draft date

Rep Pay, In One Paragraph

Per paid deal, by program: flat by debt band on Shield and ELP, a volume-tiered percentage of enrolled debt on Level Debt (final after the 2nd payment). Marketing — Test Before You Scale

Three ways to put a qualified caller in front of a closer. Transfers are the cheapest and vendor-limited; mail and social are ours to control and only earn budget once they have proven their cost per paid deal against what a deal earns.

Purchased transfers

  • Fill every seat with transfers first — about $75 per paid deal at today's close rate
  • Buffer mix ($8 / $14 / $55) tuned monthly against close rate by buffer
  • Every transfer billed is credit-pulled and logged; disputes filed weekly
  • Cap = what vendors can actually deliver; that cap is why mail exists

Direct mail + prescreen data

  • List from a bureau reseller (Altair CreditReady, CRS LeadsIQ, Deluxe): high revolving utilization, $20k+ unsecured, no bankruptcy
  • Firm-offer-of-credit structure through the lender partner; declines route to debt programs
  • $0.80 all-in per piece; tracking number per drop; two creatives per test
  • Planning: 1.25% response · 70% qualified · 20% close · $25k debt

Paid social

  • Retargets the mailed ZIPs only — brand familiarity drives the lower CPL
  • UTM on every ad → landing page → contact in app.krestmarketing.com
  • Planning: $35 CPL · 30% qualified · 15% close; stand-alone it does not pay — as retargeting it should

Money — Close, Reserve, Distribute

The monthly close is mechanical by design: the software produces the numbers, the CPA posts them, both members see the same screen. The reserve is not negotiable, and the waterfall decides every dollar after it.

The Monthly Close (Done By The 15th)
Day 1–5
ReconcileProgram remittances matched to deals; NSFs and cancels flagged
Day 5–8
Commission runPer rep, per deal; bonuses, SPIFFs, clawbacks; expected pay dates
Day 8–10

Day 10–15
CPA postsP&L, bank vs reserve, K-1 estimate; sent to both
Day 15
DecisionHire / invest / hold, minuted
Reserve Rule

Target = six months of operating overhead plus a chargeback buffer sized on trailing twelve months of commission paid. Below target: no hire, no new marketing, no distributions, and Shield files may be sold as buyouts to rebuild it. At or above target: the waterfall runs.

Spending Authority
  • Members are the only signers; dual control above $10,000
  • No cards, ACH credentials or wire authority to anyone else — including CPA, HR and any future sales director
  • Marketing may flex 25% over plan on the Manager's signature; beyond that, both
  • Any new account of any kind: both signatures, added to the Accounts Schedule

Clawbacks And Chargebacks
  • NSF on a first draft: the deal never counted; nothing paid, nothing to recover
  • Cancellation inside the program's window after commission was paid: reversed on the rep's next run, shown on My Deals & Pay with the reason
  • Company-level chargebacks from a program partner: booked against the reserve's buffer; buffer reviewed quarterly
  • File buyouts: one lump sum after the first cleared payment; exposure ends there — that is their purpose
What Both Members See, Always
  • Operating Model and Growth Plan (live assumptions, month-by-month)
  • Bank balance vs reserve target; distributions to date; payback remaining
  • Deals, first-pay rate, cancellation rate, occupancy, cost per deal by channel
